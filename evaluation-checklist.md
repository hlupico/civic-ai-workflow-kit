# Civic AI Evaluation Checklist

*Civic AI Workflow Kit — /evaluations*  
*Last updated: May 2026*

---

Use this checklist when evaluating AI output in a government workflow context. It is designed for practitioner use to assess whether an AI tool is ready to support real staff in a real workflow.  It's not for technical model evaluations.

---

## 1. Accuracy

- [ ] Did the AI correctly identify the issues that were present?
- [ ] Did it miss any issues that a human reviewer would have caught?
- [ ] Did it surface the right information from the right source documents?
- [ ] Were specific values (numbers, names, dates, identifiers) reported accurately?

---

## 2. Hallucinations

- [ ] Did the AI report anything that was not present in the source documents?
- [ ] Did it make claims it could not have known from the submitted materials?
- [ ] Were citations to source documents accurate (right document, right field)?

---

## 3. Calibration

- [ ] Were severity levels appropriate? (Minor variations vs. hard errors)
- [ ] Were items flagged as reviewer notes when they required human judgment to resolve?
- [ ] Did the AI avoid over-flagging items that were not actually issues?
- [ ] Were clean results reported accurately — not just the flags?

---

## 4. Trustworthiness

- [ ] Can a reviewer trust an all-clear result, or do they feel the need to re-check?
- [ ] Is the output traceable — can the reviewer see exactly where each finding came from?
- [ ] Is the AI transparent about what it could and could not check?
- [ ] Are the limitations of the check clearly communicated?

---

## 5. Operational usefulness

- [ ] Does the output format match how staff actually work through a review?
- [ ] Can a reviewer scan the output efficiently, or does it require close reading?
- [ ] Is the output specific enough to support downstream actions (e.g., drafting a deficiency notice)?
- [ ] Does the tool reduce cognitive load, or does it add a new verification burden?

---

## 6. Consistency

- [ ] Does the AI produce consistent results when run on the same documents multiple times?
- [ ] Are similar issues flagged at similar severity levels across different applications?
- [ ] Would two reviewers interpret the output the same way?

---

## 7. Accessibility and clarity

- [ ] Is the output understandable to staff who are not AI experts?
- [ ] Are technical findings translated into plain language a reviewer can act on?
- [ ] Is the output free of jargon that would create confusion?

---

## 8. Government context

- [ ] Does the output support human accountability for the decision?
- [ ] Are edge cases and policy gaps flagged for human judgment rather than resolved by AI?
- [ ] Is the output auditable? Can it be reviewed after the fact if a decision is contested?
- [ ] Are equity and procedural fairness considerations preserved in how the tool operates?

---

## Scoring guidance

This is not a pass/fail checklist. Use it to structure honest evaluation of what the tool did well, where it fell short, and what would need to change before deployment in a real workflow.

Document your findings. The goal is to build an honest record of capability and limitation.
